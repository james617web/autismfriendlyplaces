# Finding Places That Fit Your Family in Florida

Finding somewhere to go can take more planning when your child has sensory needs. A place may look suitable from the outside, but noise, crowds, lighting, waiting, or unfamiliar routines can change the whole experience. Having clear information before you leave can make it easier to decide what fits your family.

[PlaceAut](https://placeaut.com/sensory-match) is made for families in Florida looking for autism-friendly places, events, and resources. You can use PlaceAut to discover options, save places for later, and find information that supports a more comfortable visit. Parents can also recommend places for review, helping other families find useful local information.

## Use PlaceAut to Match Sensory Preferences

Every child responds differently to a new place. One child may enjoy an active setting, while another may need a quieter space, more predictable surroundings, or fewer sensory demands. General labels do not always tell you whether a place will work for your family.

The PlaceAut Sensory Match Quiz uses a few quick questions about your child's sensory preferences. It then matches those preferences with autism-friendly places in Florida. You can start with what your child needs now, rather than trying to judge every location from a general description.

You can find the quiz through PlaceAut. The matching process can help you make a more informed choice before planning an outing. It is not about finding one type of place for every child. It is about noticing what tends to work for your child and using that information when you choose where to go.

## Find Information Before You Visit

PlaceAut includes more than places to visit. Families can look for events, social stories, and other resources that may help with planning. Social stories can be useful when your child benefits from knowing what to expect. Event information can help you decide whether an activity suits your family before you make plans.

The platform also includes information from businesses that list and manage their places. Some listings can include in-person family evaluations for certified listings. This gives families another part of the picture when they are deciding where to spend time.

Parents can recommend a place for review if they know somewhere that may support autistic children and their families. Businesses can list their places as well, which helps keep local options in one place. Families can also check available options for free tickets when those are offered.

Start with your child's sensory preferences, save the places that seem suitable, and check the details before you go. A little planning can make the next outing feel more manageable for everyone.
